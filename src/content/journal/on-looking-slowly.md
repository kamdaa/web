There is a particular hour in the studio when the light stops being useful and starts being interesting. The work changes colour without anyone touching it. I have learned to keep a chair facing the wall for exactly this reason.

Looking is not passive. It is a kind of slow editing — the eye keeps proposing things and the mind keeps refusing them, until something survives.

Most of my paintings are made in this refusal. I put a shape down, live with it for a week, and then take most of it away. What remains is not the shape I intended. It is the shape that stayed.

> A painting is finished when the room agrees with it.

I used to think this was indecision. Now I think it is the actual work: making a surface that can hold attention rather than demand it.

If you stand in front of a piece for longer than feels comfortable, something usually happens. A colour you didn't notice begins to organise the rest. A line that looked arbitrary turns out to be holding the whole thing up. This is not a trick. It is just what happens when you give an image time.

The journal is a place to keep these small observations, so they don't get lost between one series and the next.
