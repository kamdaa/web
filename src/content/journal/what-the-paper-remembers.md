Paper is a difficult companion. It records every hesitation — the second pass, the corrected edge, the place where the brush was too wet. Canvas lets you build and bury. Paper keeps a diary.

I have come to prefer it for that reason.

The works on paper in this collection were made at the window, usually in the first hour of the day. They are small enough to hold, which changes the way you make decisions. There is no room for a grand gesture, so the gestures become quiet and deliberate.

A few things I have learned:

- Work standing up. The body knows when a composition is unbalanced before the eye admits it.
- Keep the first wash. It is almost always the most honest one.
- Stop before the paper gets tired. You will feel it — the surface starts to resist.

Gouache is unforgiving in the best way. It dries flat and fast, so a passage either happens now or becomes a different painting. I like that urgency. It keeps me from being precious.

These pieces are not studies for larger works. They are complete thoughts, just smaller than the ones on canvas.
